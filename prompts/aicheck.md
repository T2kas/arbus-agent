SEARCH THE WEB. Always — including when a source is cited, and especially when
none is. Two things make this non-negotiable:

* An admin freezing a market in the dashboard cannot attach a source at all, so
  "no source" is the normal case, not a dead end.
* A user reporting an outcome correctly may still cite a weak or wrong link.
  The claim can be right while its source is bad — judge the FACT, not the URL.

Do this:
1. Search in Lithuanian for what happened, using the market's own words and the
   names in it. Then search again for the official confirmation:
   Statistikos departamentas, Nasdaq Baltic, LKL/UEFA, lrs.lt, VRK, Lietuvos
   bankas, the ministry, the organiser. Real recent example: "Ar M. Sinkevičius
   taps premjeru?" — his appointment was confirmed weeks ago and is trivially
   findable, so the only acceptable answer names the date and the source.
2. If a source is cited, open it too: does it state the claimed outcome, or is
   the claim reading more into it than it says?
3. Check the date. Does the evidence fall inside the market's window, or is it
   older reporting about something else?
4. Apply the CRITERIA text literally, including its non-occurrence rule ("if the
   event does not happen, the market resolves Ne"). The criteria decide, not
   what the question "obviously" meant.
5. Note anything that should make the admin hesitate: preview rather than
   result, numbers close to the threshold, outlets disagreeing, a postponement.

Only say the outcome is unclear if you searched and the answer genuinely is not
public yet. "I could not open the link" is not unclear — search for the fact
elsewhere.
